# Dear Tomorrow · Pre-launch QA audit

**26 November 2026 — No-go for public launch. Continue protected review.** The creative tools work well enough to make real keepsakes. The strongest evidence is an actual two-account test: a partner could read one deliberately shared Memory Book and its photo, could not read the owner’s second book, and lost access after revocation. The most urgent defect is on the other side of that same interaction. When a cloud user adds a photo, Studio explicitly tells them it stays only on their device.

**First priorities**

1. **Tell customers where their photos and drafts go.** Correct the cloud photo picker, the device-storage banners on cloud Letters and Tree, and the homepage’s “unsaved demo” wording. A fictional guest book persisted across a complete browser restart. Misleading disclosure can cause harm even when access controls work. DT-QA-01 and 05.
2. **Make the interface readable.** The 390px accessibility scan found 38 contrast failures across all 11 sampled pages. Some are body text, including homepage paragraphs at 3.66:1. The clean About build requested Playfair Display in CSS but drew Georgia. Verify actual type delivery before approving final line breaks. DT-QA-02, 03 and 20.
3. **Run the release-origin customer journey.** The protected domain serves staging 8f1dce4; the newer main candidate f3aef74 was tested locally. Small cloud saves on localhost cannot validate large HTTPS transfers through Vercel. A generated recovery link reaching the correct account does not demonstrate email arriving in a real inbox. DT-QA-08 and 09.
4. **Establish recovery and public disclosures.** Demonstrate a matched restore of documents, private media and permissions. Establish alerts, clarify retention and owner deletion, and have the privacy and terms information reviewed before inviting the public. DT-QA-10, 11, 12 and 16.

### What is worth protecting

- **The physical book is the standout feature.** The ribbon, paper surfaces, page turns and all seven Little Surprises work in the actual reader. All three reading environments worked. A 100-page Studio fixture with 75 objects on one page accepted real edits, a recording and an uploaded WAV. Its export contained the transcript and voice asset. One measured turn had a 43 ms p95; most were substantially smoother.
- **The personal tree gives each memory a navigable place.** Two memories on the same leap day remained distinct from calendar to leaf. A 2,400-memory synthetic backup reached SVG paint in 293 ms. Keep the illustrated growth and the painted landscape. The experimental tree has impressive measured graphics performance, but the golden painted crown is more coherent at a glance.
- **Private sharing is selective and reversible for future reads.** The fictional partner, unrelated user, explicit revocation and disconnect tests carry more weight than the visual presence of a lock icon. No confirmed P0 disclosure or data loss was identified in those tested paths. Revocation cannot recall a previously downloaded file.
- **Letters have a clear physical vocabulary.** Writing, sealing, reopening, attaching media, restoring backups and resolving a conflicting letter draft all worked. The airport route and six envelopes communicate a journey without requiring live location. Product copy must distinguish an unlock from proactive delivery.

### Scope and verification

The documented feature inventory preceded browser testing. The audit used an optimized build of main, an isolated R&D worktree/branch comparison, authorized development Supabase, and read-only inspection of the staging deployment configuration. It produced **176 real browser route/viewport measurements** (Edge 132, Firefox 22, WebKit 22), **93 passing model/policy/transport tests**, successful lint, typecheck and production build, and fresh hosted API/browser results. WebKit raised fetch errors on rapid navigation despite successful rendering. Current staging protection is active for all deployments.
